Copper and the Electric Vehicle Transition (synthetic briefing note, for hackathon evaluation only)

Copper is a core input for electric vehicles: an average battery-electric vehicle uses
roughly 83 kilograms of copper, compared to about 23 kilograms in a conventional
internal-combustion vehicle, because of the electric motor windings, battery
interconnects, and charging infrastructure.

Global refined copper demand from the automotive sector was approximately 2.8 million
tonnes in 2024. Industry analysts project this could rise to approximately 4.6 million
tonnes by 2030 if electric vehicle adoption continues at its current trajectory, driven
primarily by growth in China, the European Union, and the United States.

Zambia is Africa's second-largest copper producer. National output reached 820,000
tonnes in 2024, according to the country's Ministry of Mines and Minerals Development,
up from 763,000 tonnes in 2023. The government has stated a target of reaching 3 million
tonnes of annual copper production by 2031 through new mine investment and the
reopening of previously idled sites.

Analysts note that reaching this target depends on sustained foreign investment, stable
electricity supply for processing plants, and continued access to export infrastructure
through regional rail and port corridors. A prolonged drought in 2024 reduced
hydroelectric output and forced several mines to curtail production for part of the year,
illustrating one of the risks to the growth trajectory.
